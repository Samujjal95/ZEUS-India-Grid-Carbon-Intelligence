In plain terms: Every time you use electricity in India, some CO₂ is emitted. The mix of coal, gas, hydro, wind, and solar changes every 15 minutes — so the carbon footprint changes too. ZEUS measures that footprint in real time and forecasts it 48 hours ahead, so you can report it accurately and reduce it practically.

Source & uncertainty:
· CEA CO₂ Baseline Database v21.0 (Nov 2025) — covers FY 2024-25 — Weighted Average Grid Emission Rate (incl. RES, captive) 0.7117 tCO₂/MWh = 711.7 g/kWh (rounded to 712 g/kWh as our reference)
· CEA emission factors carry ±3-5% uncertainty (per CEA convention & ISO 14064-1 best practice)
· Thermal generation mix per latest CEA monthly installed capacity report: ~97% coal + ~3% lignite (~217 GW coal vs ~6.6 GW lignite as of mid-2025). CEA's 1.052 factor reflects historical generation-weighted average from FY 2024-25 PLF ratios (lignite plants run lower PLF, so generation share drops further to ~98:2)

Other assumptions:
· Imports = zero (small Bhutan hydro <2% of generation, conservative exclusion)
· BESS round-trip losses not modelled. India's operational grid-scale BESS was ~0.5 GWh as of June 2025 (per IEEFA & Energy-Storage News); deployment scaling rapidly through 2026 — figure verifiable against NLDC daily reports
· State intensity = REMC actuals + CEA latest published capacity weights
· Hydro not modelled at state level (conservative — hydro-heavy states under-represented)
· T&D losses NOT added to Scope 2 factor (CEA bus-bar convention; Indian GHG Programme aligned)
3. Intensity Formula
// Per 15-min block t: intensity(t) = Σ[gen_fuel(t) × EF_fuel] ──────────── total_generation(t) // × 1000 → g CO₂/kWh
In plain terms: Multiply each fuel's output (MW) by its CO₂ factor, divide by total generation. Result = grams of CO₂ per kWh consumed.

5. Model Architecture
Algorithm: XGBoost gradient boosting
Horizons: 1h, 6h, 12h, 24h, 48h ahead
Models: Separate model per horizon (direct forecasting, no error accumulation)
Tuning: Optuna Bayesian optimisation · 100 trials per horizon
CV: 5-fold rolling-origin cross-validation (time-series safe — no future leakage)
Intervals: Conformal prediction (split conformal, marginal coverage guaranteed)
Solar weighting: 2× sample weight for 06:00–17:00 IST hours
6. Feature Engineering
~95 features per horizon

Autoregressive lags (per-horizon, no leakage):
· 1h forecast: lags at t−1, t−2, t−3, t−6, t−12, t−24, t−48, t−168
· 24h forecast: lags at t−24, t−48, t−72, t−168 only

Weather (19 zones · 15 vars each):
Solar irradiance, cloud cover, wind speed at 100m, temperature
Filtered via mutual information (MI > 0.005) + correlation (r < 0.95)

Calendar: Cyclic sin/cos (hour, month, DOW, day-of-year), monsoon flag, solar-hour flag
Holidays: 54 Indian festivals across FY 25-26 and FY 26-27 combined, with impact weights
REMC: Day-ahead scheduled vs actual, curtailment, forecast error %
Time decay: exp(−days_old / 365) sample weighting

9. Scope 2 Calculator
Standard: GHG Protocol Corporate Standard · Location-based method · BRSR Core Schedule D compliant

Formula:
Annual Scope 2 (tCO₂/year) = Annual consumption (kWh) × EF_avg (g/kWh) ÷ 1,000,000

Emission factor used: The calculator above uses the period-specific factor based on your selection (FY 2025-26: 755.1 g/kWh from 362 days; FY 2026-27 YTD: 782.5 g/kWh; rolling 12 months: 754.7 g/kWh; all data: 757.8 g/kWh).
For BRSR Core annual filing of FY 2025-26, the default selection is the strict-period FY 2025-26 mean (755.1 g/kWh). Auditors comparing to CEA v21.0 will use the FY 2024-25 baseline (712 g/kWh) and accept either a strict-period match or a ±5% combined uncertainty justification.

CEA baseline comparison: 712 g/kWh (CEA CO₂ Baseline Database v21.0, FY 2024-25). The live ZEUS factor is 757.8 g/kWh — the difference quantifies whether the actual grid has been cleaner or dirtier than the published static baseline.

Max Shift Saving: Theoretical upper bound on CO₂ reduction if 100% of input kWh moves from peak window (834 g/kWh avg) to solar window (626 g/kWh avg). Use as ceiling, not target — physically, only ~15–25% of monthly consumption can fit into a 4-hour daily window.

What this does NOT include:
· Transmission & distribution (T&D) losses — CEA factor is bus-bar (generation), Indian convention is to NOT add T&D for Scope 2 (handled separately)
· Market-based instruments (RECs, captive PPAs, CFE certificates)
· Temporal matching finer than monthly
· 24/7 carbon-free energy matching (v0.3)
· Scope 3 emissions (upstream fuel)
· Compliance reporting for jurisdictions outside India
10. Load Shift Opportunity
What it calculates: Financial and carbon savings from shifting flexible loads from the evening peak window (17:00–22:00) to the solar midday window (10:00–14:00 IST).

⚠ Peak windows vary by state. ZEUS uses an evening-peak-only assumption (17:00–22:00) that matches Maharashtra MERC, Karnataka KERC, and most central/north state ToU schedules. Tamil Nadu (TNERC FY 2026) has a dual-peak structure: 06:00–10:00 morning peak + 18:00–22:00 evening peak (both billed at +25% on energy charges). For TN industrial consumers, your real savings are higher than ZEUS shows because morning-peak shifting is also valuable. Andhra Pradesh, Telangana use 06:00–10:00 + 18:00–22:00 as well. Verify your DISCOM's ToU schedule before relying on this calculator.
Window definitions (used in calculator):
· Peak window: 17:00–22:00 IST (blocks 17, 18, 19, 20, 21) — 5 hourly blocks
· Solar window: 10:00–14:00 IST (blocks 10, 11, 12, 13) — 4 hourly blocks
Both averaged across the last 30 days of IEX DAM data.

1. Energy cost saving:
Saving = (P_peak − P_solar) × MWh_shifted P_peak = avg DAM price 17–22h = ₹7,481/MWh P_solar = avg DAM price 10–14h = ₹1,123/MWh MWh_shifted = MW × h/day × 30.42 days/month

Pricing assumption: IEX Day-Ahead Market (DAM) prices. Only directly applicable to Open Access consumers (typically ≥1 MW, connected at 33 kV+). DISCOM tariff consumers should substitute their time-of-use rate differential.

2. Demand charge saving:
Saving = kVA_shifted × 0.50 × ₹450/kVA/month kVA = MW × 1000 / 0.95 (HT power factor) 0.50 = peak coincidence factor (conservative) ₹450/kVA = typical HT demand charge (India)

Power factor 0.95: HT consumers in India are mandated to maintain PF ≥ 0.95 (PF correction penalty applies if lower). Typical industrial PF ranges 0.95–0.99.
Peak coincidence 0.50: Demand charges are billed on the maximum demand recorded in any 15-min block of the month. We assume only 50% of shifted MW reduces actual peak demand — the user's load profile may not perfectly align with the grid peak window. Real-world coincidence ranges 0.30–0.80 depending on operations.
₹450/kVA/month: National midpoint estimate. Actual verified state rates (FY 2025-26 / 2026): Tamil Nadu ₹608/kVA (TNERC), Maharashtra ₹450–540/kVA (MERC), Karnataka ₹280–330/kVA (KERC), Delhi ₹325–450/kVA (DERC). Verify with your DISCOM bill.
Demand charges are typically 30–50% of HT electricity bills, often larger than energy savings.

3. Carbon saving:
Saving (tCO₂) = (I_peak − I_solar) / 1000 × MWh_shifted I_peak = 834 g/kWh (17–22h avg, 30 days) I_solar = 626 g/kWh (10–14h avg, 30 days)
Unit check: (g/kWh) / 1000 × MWh = (kg/kWh × 1000 kWh/MWh × MWh) / 1000 = tCO₂

Important caveats:
· Peak windows differ by state (see warning above — especially TN, AP, Telangana with dual peaks)
· Window prices are 30-day averages of hourly DAM — daily savings vary
· Demand rate (₹450/kVA) is illustrative — verified state rates listed above
· Peak coincidence (0.5) is a conservative average — varies by load profile
· Power factor (0.95) is the regulatory minimum for HT consumers
· Large shifts (>100 MW) change the grid mix itself — not modelled
· BESS round-trip losses (~10–15%) not included
· Not applicable to residential or small commercial (LT) consumers
