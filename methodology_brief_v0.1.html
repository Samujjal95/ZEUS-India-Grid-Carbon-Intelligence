Measurement Methodology
Date: May 2026 Version: 0.1-draft Coverage: All-India · Apr 2025 – present Granularity: 15-minute

1. Purpose

In plain terms: Every time you use electricity in India, some CO₂ is emitted to generate it. The mix of coal, gas, hydro, wind, and solar on the grid changes every 15 minutes — so the carbon footprint of your electricity changes too. This product measures that footprint in real time and historically, so you can report it accurately and reduce it practically.

Technically, this document describes the methodology used to compute the carbon intensity of electricity consumed from the Indian national grid, expressed in grammes of CO₂ per kilowatt-hour (g CO₂/kWh), at 15-minute granularity. The metric is the location-based grid average emission intensity, aligned with the GHG Protocol Corporate Standard Scope 2 Guidance (2015, Chapter 6) — the method required for BRSR, CDP, and CCTS disclosures.

Primary use case: Scope 2 location-based GHG reporting, 24/7 carbon-free energy (CFE) matching for data centres and large consumers, and CCTS/Indian Carbon Market baseline calculations.

4. Calculation Formula

// For each 15-minute block t: intensity(t) = Σ [ generation_fuel(t) × EF_fuel ] ───────────────────────────────────── total_generation(t) // Units: tCO₂/MWh → multiply by 1,000 for g CO₂/kWh (API output unit)

In plain terms: For each 15-minute period, we multiply each fuel source's output (in MW) by how much CO₂ that fuel emits per unit of electricity, then divide by total generation. The result is how many grams of CO₂ were emitted per kWh consumed from the grid during that period.

5. Documented Assumptions

THERMAL blending
PSP reports coal and lignite as a single THERMAL column. National installed capacity (CEA Mar 2025): coal 239 GW, lignite ~7 GW. Applied weights: 95% coal × 1.04 + 5% lignite × 1.28 = 1.052 tCO₂/MWh.
Cross-border imports
Net transnational exchange (Bhutan, Nepal, Bangladesh) is small (<3 GW vs ~230 GW peak) and treated as zero-emission per CEA Tool v7.0 §10–11 option (b): zero factor for countries without published grid EF.
Miscellaneous generation
Small diesel generators, naphtha peakers, and biomass co-generation units grouped together. Conservative factor of 0.70 tCO₂/MWh applied. This category is less than 1.5% of total generation — its effect on the overall intensity figure is under 0.5%. We deliberately err high so the intensity is never understated.
Storage (BESS)
India's operational BESS fleet is <1 GW as of 2026. Round-trip losses not modelled. Impact on national intensity <0.3%. Will be incorporated in v0.2 as fleet scales.

7. Sanity Check — Alignment with CEA Reference

712 g/kWh
CEA reference for FY 2024-25: Weighted Average Grid Emission Rate including RES & Captive = 0.712 tCO₂/MWh
Source: CEA CO₂ Baseline Database v21.0, Results sheet Row 14
Our computed FY 2025-26 mean: ~755 g/kWh — within ±8% of CEA reference (FY difference accounts for deviation; more solar in FY25-26 pulls intensity lower over the full year).
Why our number (755 g/kWh) is higher than CEA's reference (712 g/kWh): CEA's 712 is the average for the full FY 2024-25 (April 2024 – March 2025), which includes the monsoon season (Jul–Sep) when hydro and wind generation peaks and grid intensity drops sharply. Our data starts from April 2025 — summer months when thermal plants run at maximum load and intensity is highest. Once our data covers a full year including monsoon, the annual mean will align closely with the CEA reference. The ±8% difference is expected and documented, not an error.
